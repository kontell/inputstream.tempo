Tempo for Video

Can the add-on's pitch-corrected rate control cover audio and video, so kofin SyncPlay gets a fine speed/position actuator that does not depend on Kodi's Sync playback to display setting?

Read against Kodi 21 ref/kodi-omega-full and Kodi 22 ref/kodi-piers-full (22.0b1+731) inputstream.tempo 21.3.11 (Omega branch) kofin docs/syncplay-drift-shakedown.md Nothing executed — every Kodi claim below is source-read; §8 lists what still has to be measured

Yes — and it is the only add-on-level actuator that works with the display clock off.

The add-on already keeps two clocks for audio: packet pts at output rate for ActiveAE, and content time for the OSD through a dynamic ptsStart. Video does not need decoding — its packets need their timestamps projected through the same content→output map the audio path already maintains. Everything Kodi does downstream (audio-master clock, renderer following the clock, OSD, external subtitles, accurate seeks) keys off two fields the add-on controls: ptsStart/time_offset and startpts. So this is timestamp arithmetic plus four pieces of plumbing, not a new pipeline.

What it buys kofin

  • A rate actuator on the configuration the shakedown found tightest — display clock off, where all three members tracked real time within ±200 ppm — with no panel-imposed bias to fight.
  • Pitch-corrected, and invisible to Kodi's tempo OSD: Kodi's own tempo stays 1.0, so Player.IsTempo never flickers.
  • Fine position trims where seeks cannot land: transcodes snap to segment boundaries; a 2 % pulse for 10 s moves 200 ms smoothly.
  • Audio-only items get the same actuator for free — that is today's PAPlayer path.

What it costs

  • Passthrough is lost whenever the add-on is in the path: it hands Kodi PCM f32. Scope it to SyncPlay sessions.
  • A change takes effect only after the already-queued packets play out: ≈4 s on Piers by default, 8 s on Omega. The controller must be built for dead time.
  • The position Kodi reports is biased by (r−1)·L while a nudge runs unless the add-on compensates — §4.3 shows how.
  • A sustained rate change can make Kodi re-detect the frame rate and rebuild its renderer — a brief hiccup; a display-mode switch is unlikely.

Recommendation. Build it in three phases — add-on core, kofin wiring, rig measurement (§7) — gate it on direct play first, and treat Piers as the primary target: its demux queue is a user setting, which is the lever that turns a 9 s dead time into 2 s.

1. Why this lever

Kodi's native Player.SetTempo exists only while videoplayer.usedisplayasclock is on (VideoPlayer.cpp:4917–4925; Piers CanTempo() :5765), and that setting slaves the media clock to the panel. The shakedown's Gate 0 measured what that costs on the three Piers members:

MemberDisplay clock onDisplay clock off
Bravia (23.976 on 50 Hz)+42 764 ppm−70 ppm
Pixel 7 Pro−6 287 ppm+204 ppm
Galaxy Tab S5e+4 761 ppm+21 ppm

The actuator kofin needs exists only in the mode that ruins the clock it is trying to correct; with the mode off the clock is already good to 200 ppm and there is no actuator. The controller was removed for that reason (playback.py, class docstring; shakedown §10).

An inputstream add-on changes the content instead of the clock. With the display-clock setting off and a non-realtime stream, VideoPlayer runs audio in SYNC_DISCON: the audio sink's progress is the clock (VideoPlayerAudio.cpp:133–138). Feed that sink PCM that has been time-stretched by atempo, stamp the packets at output rate, and the clock keeps its ±200 ppm honesty while the media runs 3 % faster. Video only has to carry timestamps that agree.

3. How Kodi will treat it

Every item here was read in the Kodi 21 tree and checked against Piers; where the two differ it is noted. Line numbers are Kodi 21 unless marked.

  • Clock. Display clock off + non-realtime → SYNC_DISCON; the audio sink drives CDVDClock VideoPlayerAudio.cpp:133–138. The renderer presents each picture when the clock reaches its pts. A continuous, monotone timestamp map therefore keeps A/V locked at any rate; Kodi's discontinuity detector only fires on jumps > 1 s forward or 500 ms backward (CheckContinuity).
  • OSD and getTime(). state.time = clock − ptsStart, time_offset = −ptsStart, timeMax = ptsEnd − ptsStart VideoPlayer.cpp:4858–4868; Piers :5573–5625, identical. The IDisplayTime branch is only a fallback, and CDVDDemuxClient::Read overwrites the add-on's per-packet dispTime anyway DVDDemuxClient.cpp:357–373.
  • Seeks. Relative seeks add time_offset to the clock; absolute targets are converted to output time unless the stream has IPOSTIME :2692–2703; Piers :3003–3017. With IPOSTIME, Kodi calls PosTime() then FlushDemux(), returns no startpts, and computes start = target − time_offset itself InputStreamAddon.cpp:588–600 → VideoPlayer.cpp:2716. The backward flag is not forwarded on that path, so PosTime must seek with AVSEEK_FLAG_BACKWARD or video starts a GOP late. Accurate seeks then drop packets until dts ≥ startpts, with a 20 s sanity reset :2212–2254. Resume at start uses the requested time directly :1331–1338.
  • External subtitles. Parsed at clock + time_offset and shifted by −time_offset into the clock domain VideoPlayer.cpp:1432; VideoPlayerSubtitle.cpp Process. They follow the same field the OSD uses, so getting §4.3 right fixes both.
  • Opening a stream. CVideoPlayer::OpenStream calls the add-on's OpenStream(id) first; if that returns true, Kodi re-reads the stream's properties before building the decoder hint DVDDemuxClient.cpp:731–745; VideoPlayer.cpp:3542–3600. EnableStream(false) arrives on close :3931. That ordering is what makes audio-track switching possible from inside the add-on.
  • Queues. Omega: 8 s per stream, hard-coded VideoPlayerAudio.cpp:66; VideoPlayerVideo.cpp:72. Piers: videoplayer.queuetimesize, a user setting with options 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 s, default 4 s, read in the CVideoPlayer constructor Piers VideoPlayer.cpp:775–777; settings.xml:956; PlayerSettings.cpp. Fullness is the dts span of queued packets DVDMessageQueue.cpp:310–330; the player reads ahead until a queue is full VideoPlayer.cpp:1452–1463.
  • Buffering. Add-on streams have no cache status (DVDInputStream.h:187), so after a stall CACHESTATE_FULL is released only when a queue fills (HandlePlaySpeed). An add-on that holds delivery back does not wedge this — its wall-clock cap keeps filling while playback is paused — but it does slow the recovery by up to the queue depth, and loses its lead bound afterwards (§4.5).
  • Frame-rate detection. CalcFrameRate runs regardless of the display-clock setting (gated only by videofpsdetect), tolerance 0.01 fps, measurement windows 1→2→4→…→64 s, and it is restarted on every flush — i.e. every seek VideoPlayerVideo.cpp:1009–1080, :474. A changed rate flows into CRenderManager::Configure, which tears down and recreates the renderer RenderManager.cpp:81–95, 157–171 and, with Adjust refresh rate on, re-runs mode selection :879–897. A mode only switches on an exact 0.01 Hz refresh match Resolution.cpp FindResolutionFromWhitelist, which a nudged rate essentially never produces.
  • Passthrough. Allowed only with the display clock off and no resampling VideoPlayerAudio.cpp:653–655 — moot here, because the add-on delivers PCM.
  • Post-seek discard. The add-on's SeekTime reads and frees packets until it knows a pts; Kodi's own CDVDDemuxFFmpeg::SeekTime does exactly the same. Parity, not a regression.

4. Design

The shape of the change, as a picture first:

4.1 One map for every stream

Define M: content → output, piecewise-linear with slope 1/rk in each segment k, where a segment starts each time the tempo changes. The audio path already walks this map — m_tempoOutputPts advances by the output duration, m_tempoContentPts by output × r. Video and subtitle packets then get:

pts'      = M(pts)
dts'      = M(dts)           // monotone map, so dts' ≤ pts' is preserved
duration' = duration / r_k   // text subtitles carry their display time here too

Keep a short list of segment anchors (contentk, outputk, rk): packets in flight are interleaved within about a second of the audio, so a video packet arriving just after a tempo change may still belong to the previous segment, and the list resolves that exactly. Accuracy is not a concern: atempo's analysis window (~40 ms) and codec priming add a constant A/V offset of tens of milliseconds, inside Kodi's own tolerance, and the transient a tempo change introduces is window × Δr ≈ 2 ms, which reverses when the rate returns.

4.2 The continuity rule

Let Δ = content − output. Δ must change only while r ≠ 1 — never at a seek, never at a flush. Today DemuxFlush zeroes the counters and SeekTime re-anchors output to the first audio packet's dts FFmpegStream.cpp:309–318, 3070–3080, which resets Δ to 0. That is harmless while only audio is shifted, but it breaks the IPOSTIME path: Kodi computes the drop-until point as target − time_offset using the Δ it last saw, so new packets must continue from exactly that Δ. Anchor post-seek packets at output = content − Δreported, anchor on the first packet of any mapped stream (today only audio anchors, and video usually arrives first), and the OSD stops blinking at seeks as a side effect.

4.3 Report time at the playing point, not the demux head

The current ptsStart is Δ of the last packet popped — the demux head, which runs L seconds ahead of what is playing (L ≈ the queue depth in steady state). Δ grows at (r−1) per second while a nudge runs, so Kodi's reported position is wrong by:

Rate rL = 2 sL = 4 s (Piers default)L = 8 s (Omega)
1.0120 ms40 ms80 ms
1.0240 ms80 ms160 ms
1.0360 ms120 ms240 ms

That bias ramps in over L seconds, holds at (r−1)·L, and ramps out over L after the rate returns — and it is the same size as the errors SyncPlay is trying to close. A controller that reads position during a nudge would chase it. Two complementary fixes:

  • In the add-on: keep a ring of (output_pts, Δ) recorded at each pop and have GetTimes() report the Δ for output_head − Q, where Q is the queue depth passed as a property (inputstream.tempo.queue_secs; kofin supplies the Piers setting, 8 for Omega). The residual is (r−1)·|Lactual − Q|: zero in steady state, and during refills after a seek Δ is constant anyway because r = 1. This also makes external subtitles land on the playing frame during a nudge.
  • In kofin: treat nudges as pulses and measure between them — after r has been 1 for Q + 1 s — rather than running a continuous loop against a biased reading.

4.4 Audio-track switching

Advertise every audio stream as pcm_f32le with the source codec, bitrate and bit depth kept for the OSD, as the tempo stream does now. Language, default flag and channel count stay truthful, so Kodi's own selection logic is unaffected. In OpenStream(id) for an audio stream: tear down the pipeline for the previous track, build decoder and graph for the new one, set AVDISCARD_ALL on the others, and return true so Kodi re-reads the properties before creating the decoder (§3). EnableStream(false) confirms the old track is gone. Kodi flushes around a track change anyway, so the map re-anchors under the continuity rule like any seek.

4.5 Dead time and pacing

A write to the tempo file is heard only after three delays: the poll, everything Kodi has already queued, and the sink.

Three consequences for the design:

  • Never block the player loop. Under VideoPlayer, DemuxRead runs on the thread that handles pause and seek messages; today's throttle sleeps up to a second there. Cap any wait at ~10 ms and return.
  • The add-on's lead bound is best-effort. The 2 s throttle holds from a clean start, but after any pause or buffering stall Kodi's queue fills to its maximum and the add-on has no safe way to re-establish a smaller lead without observing playback, which it cannot. Keep the throttle so Kodi's CACHESTATE_FULL still releases (it fills at wall rate while playback is paused), and design the controller for Q + 1 s.
  • On Piers, use the setting. videoplayer.queuetimesize at 1–2 s is ample for LAN direct play — Kodi's file cache sits below the demuxer in curl open mode, so network jitter is still absorbed — and it is the one lever that brings dead time to ≈2 s. It is read when the player object is constructed, so kofin should set it before a session and restore it after; whether a stop is needed between the two is in §8.

4.6 IPC between kofin and the add-on

  • Per-session tempo file via the existing inputstream.tempo.tempo_file property. koshelf owns special://temp/inputstream_tempo; sharing it would let an audiobook session and a SyncPlay session write over each other.
  • Poll by wall clock (mtime check every 100–250 ms) instead of every 50 reads, so latency does not depend on packet rate.
  • Write back a small state file beside it — applied tempo, the content time at which it took effect, the current Δ — so kofin can confirm a pulse landed and reason about it without guessing.
  • Leave the ACTIVE sentinel and the PgUp/PgDn keymap to koshelf. kofin never writes the sentinel, so the keys cannot change a SyncPlay member's speed.

4.7 Parity with Kodi's own demuxer

  • HDR10: map the four colour fields (§2). Static metadata already passes.
  • Dolby Vision: MP4 with dvhe/dvh1 works — Kodi's MediaCodec path reads the fourcc DVDVideoCodecAndroidMediaCodec.cpp:523–533. MKV Dolby Vision relies on hdrType, which the inputstream ABI cannot carry on 21 or 22, so it plays as plain HEVC: fine for profile 8.1 (HDR10 base layer), wrong colours for profile 5. Either exclude profile-5 MKV on Android from the tempo path or accept it for SyncPlay sessions.
  • HLS transcodes: the add-on opens HLS through ffmpeg's own demuxer (ffmpeg open mode), not Kodi's HLS handling. Start-up, duration and growing-playlist behaviour need their own check before transcodes are routed this way — and transcodes are where position pulses are worth the most, because seeks snap to segments. Phase 2.
  • Network buffering: plain http(s) opens in curl mode, through Kodi's CFile and its cache, so the user's buffer settings still apply.

4.8 Wiring it into kofin

At play time, when all of SyncPlay session, video item, direct play or direct stream, and passthrough off or the user opted in hold: set inputstream = inputstream.tempo, inputstream.tempo.tempo = 1.0, a per-session tempo_file, and queue_secs matching the Piers setting. Resume keeps working through setResumePoint — VideoPlayer seeks the demuxer before output starts VideoPlayer.cpp:1331–1338; do not set start_time, which arms the PAPlayer-specific silence hold.

The controller becomes a pulse scheduler rather than the old continuous ladder: a pulse is (r, T) with displacement (r−1)·T, |r−1| ≤ 0.03, T chosen from the measured residual; wait Q + 1 s after the pulse before trusting position; seek when the residual exceeds what a pulse can close in ~10 s (≈300 ms at 3 %), exactly the budget the shakedown computed; on transcodes, pulses are the only fine tool. A one-signed residual across several pulses still means a rate mismatch the actuator cannot reach — keep the shakedown's "stop and tell the user" follow-up.

5. Costs, limits, and where it bites

  • certainPassthrough. The add-on decodes audio to PCM f32 whenever it is the inputstream, even at 1.0×. Multichannel PCM over HDMI is fine for most setups; Atmos and DTS:X object metadata is gone, and an AVR that was bitstreaming will mute for a moment at the session start. Switching between passthrough and PCM mid-stream is not an option — a codec change makes Kodi reopen the audio stream, which is far more audible than any nudge. This is a scoping decision: SyncPlay sessions only, and a setting for passthrough households.
  • by designDead time and readout bias as in §4.5 and §4.3. Both are bounded and both are handled — but only if the controller is designed for them from the start.
  • measureCadence during a nudge. On a display matched to the content rate, a 1 % pulse drops or repeats one frame every 100 frames (~4 s at 23.976), 3 % every 33 frames. On the rig's 60 Hz panels that hides inside the existing 3:2 cadence; on a 24p-matched HTPC it is the same micro-stutter any rate mismatch produces. Short pulses keep it short.
  • measureFrame-rate re-detection. A held rate triggers a renderer rebuild once it has been stable for the current detection window. The window restarts at 1 s after every seek, so the likeliest trigger is a pulse issued in the seconds after a group seek; a few minutes into steady playback the window is 32–64 s and pulses pass under it. What a rebuild looks like on the MediaCodec surface renderer is the thing to measure (§8); a display-mode switch is not a realistic outcome.
  • acceptDolby Vision in MKV on Android loses its DV signalling (§4.7).
  • inheritedKodi's own seek quirks — the post-seek discard, the segment snapping of transcodes — are unchanged by this route.
  • maintenanceFork distance. FFmpegStream.cpp differs from upstream ffmpegdirect by ~720 lines already, and the video path means upstream's video fixes now matter (upstream is at 22.2.4 with recent catchup/timeshift fixes, one of them yours). Budget for periodic rebasing.
  • neutralCPU. Decoding one audio track and running atempo is a few percent of one ARM core; video is not touched. Worth one reading on the Tab with a 5.1 E-AC-3 track, nothing more.

6. Alternatives considered

OptionWhy not, or why not yet
Kodi's own tempo with the display clock onMeasured and rejected: the precondition imposes 0.5–4.3 % rate error on the rig, up to 14× the controller's authority, and it is not configurable away on Android (shakedown §10).
Patch Kodi to allow tempo without the display clockTempo needs the audio resampled to a clock that is not the audio; that is precisely what display-clock mode is. A new "resample while audio is master" mode is real VideoPlayer work, version-bound, and unavailable to anyone on a release build for a year or more. The inputstream route works on today's 21 and 22.
Seek-only (status quo)Accurate on direct play, impossible on transcodes, and every correction is a visible cut. It remains the right tool above ~300 ms.
inputstream.adaptiveNo rate control, and it does not open plain files or Jellyfin's static streams.
Report the stream as realtime to get SYNC_RESAMPLERealtime streams are excluded from tempo outright (!realtime in cantempo), and the mode also disables passthrough and seeking. Dead end.

7. Plan and effort

Three phases, each with something that can be measured before the next starts. Estimates assume one person who knows both code bases.

A · Add-on core

≈ 2 weeks incl. Linux + Android builds

  • Segment list and M(·); project video/subtitle pts, dts, duration
  • Continuity rule across seek and flush; anchor on the first mapped packet
  • IPOSTIME + PosTime with AVSEEK_FLAG_BACKWARD; output-domain startpts on the legacy path too
  • ptsStart at head − Q; queue_secs property
  • Audio re-target in OpenStream; all audio streams advertised as PCM
  • Wall-clock poll; per-session file; state write-back; no long sleeps in DemuxRead
  • Colour-field mapping; service props for video; disable the silence hold when a real video stream exists

  • Route SyncPlay video items (direct play/stream) through the add-on behind a setting; passthrough guard
  • Per-session tempo file; Piers queue-size set/restore
  • Pulse scheduler with Q-aware measurement windows; seek above the pulse budget; rate-mismatch give-up
  • Log lines for pulse issued / applied / residual, same style as the shakedown's

8. What to measure first

  1. The actuator moves the clock. A +3 % write must steepen the Player.GetProperties time slope by ≈ +30 000 ppm on each box, the way Gate 0 proved SetTempo did — and this time with the display clock off.
  2. A/V stays locked. Photo the frame counter and record the beep on two members during and after a 3 % pulse; target ≤ 1 frame. This is the claim the whole design rests on.
  3. Readout bias with and without the head − Q correction, against the external sampler.
  4. Renderer rebuild visibility on the Bravia, Pixel and Tab: a 20 s 3 % hold issued 2 s after a seek, then again after 3 minutes of steady play. Look for the CRenderManager::Configure - change configuration log line and whatever the screen does.
  5. Seek landing through the add-on versus the internal demuxer on direct play, using _seek_and_settle's own tolerance.
  6. Mid-play audio-track switch — sync afterwards, and the OSD's codec label.
  7. Dead time per configuration (Omega 8 s; Piers 4 s and 1 s) from file write to slope change.
  8. HDR10 on the Bravia with the colour fields mapped; then a DV MKV to confirm the fallback is as described.
  9. HLS transcode start-up, reported duration and position pulses — the gate for phase 2.
  10. Open questions to settle along the way: whether changing videoplayer.queuetimesize needs a player stop before it applies; whether Omega is worth more than "best effort" given every rig member runs Piers; and the DV-profile-5 policy.
